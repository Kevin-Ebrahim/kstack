# Quieter

Reduce the sources of visual competition rather than making every element small, pale, or identical. A reading surface and a campaign can need different levels of restraint.

## Diagnose

Inspect saturation, competing contrast, heavy type, decorative layers, large scale jumps, animation, and density. Identify the primary task or message and the few elements that should retain emphasis. Use existing product context and tokens; ask only about a material unresolved choice.

## Refine

- Reduce unnecessary color variety or saturation while preserving semantic roles and brand commitments. There is no required saturation percentage or accent quota.
- Let space, alignment, and deliberate weight changes carry hierarchy. Preserve readable body type, clear controls, and visible focus.
- Remove redundant borders, shadows, textures, and containers; retain those that communicate grouping or state.
- Remove purposeless motion. For useful feedback, reduce distance or intensity without making it sluggish or hard to perceive. Respect reduced-motion preferences and the project's motion language.
- Bring spacing and alignment into a coherent rhythm while retaining purposeful density differences.

Do not lower text or control contrast to achieve a quieter appearance. The strongest anchor should remain easy to find, and simplification should not remove useful actions or information.

## Verify

Inspect the rendered target at representative sizes and themes. Exercise primary actions and keyboard navigation. Compare reading comfort, hierarchy, and brand continuity with the baseline; distinguish observed improvements from subjective judgments. For a motion-specific pass, use [remove-animations](../remove-animations/SKILL.md).
